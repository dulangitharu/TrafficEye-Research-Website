TrafficEyeTraffic Monitoring & Green Light Support System

Individual research component of the Smart Emergency Traffic Light Control System · Department of Information Technology, Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology

TrafficEye analyses recorded video from a calibrated fixed camera. It detects the vehicles queued in one lane, orders them, estimates how long the queue needs to clear the stop line, and recommends a green-light duration. The traffic officer makes the final decision.

Research at a glance

How a recorded queue becomes a green-time recommendation

One research component, six steps. Discharge speeds come from a lookup table built beforehand by an offline calibration study.

6 steps
Start: a traffic officer uploads recorded video from a calibrated fixed camera, with its date and time.
  1. 01

    Video input

    Which camera view is this?

    The officer uploads a recorded clip; it is checked against two calibrated camera views.

  2. 02

    Vehicle detection

    What is on the road?

    YOLOv8n detects seven classes, including tuk-tuks and ambulances, on every 10th frame.

  3. 03

    ROI analysis

    Which vehicles are queued?

    Only vehicles inside the calibrated lane polygon are kept.

  4. 04

    Ordered queue

    Who clears first?

    Vehicles are ordered by distance to the reference line: P1, P2, P3–4, P5+.

  5. 05

    Green-time recommendation

    How long does the queue need?

    Cumulative distance ÷ calibrated speed, FIFO, plus a 3.12 s safety margin.

  6. 06

    Results & advisory

    What does the officer see?

    Recommendation, queue table, annotated video, history, and an ambulance advisory.

Emergency branch: if an ambulance is detected in the lane region, a priority green (its clearance time + 3.12 s, flagged HIGH) is shown alongside the normal recommendation.
Result: a recommended green-light duration for the officer. TrafficEye supports the decision; it does not switch traffic signals.

Introduction

About the research

Junction delays matter in Sri Lanka, where roads carry about 95 percent of passenger traffic and 97 percent of freight traffic. Green intervals that are fixed or based only on a vehicle count do not reflect how long a particular queue needs to clear. Two queues with the same number of vehicles can differ greatly in length and mix, and vehicles at the front of a queue move off differently from those behind them.

Local traffic is mixed: motorcycles, tuk-tuks, cars, buses and trucks share the same lane. TrafficEye detects these vehicles with a YOLOv8 model trained on seven classes, keeps only those inside a calibrated lane region, and orders them by their distance to the reference line.

The queue is then turned into a time. Each vehicle's cumulative distance is divided by a discharge speed measured for its class and queue position on recorded local traffic. A first-in-first-out rule and a safety margin derived from real crossing gaps give the recommended green duration.

This website covers the individual component TrafficEye: Traffic Monitoring & Green Light Support System, developed by T. A. D. T. Jayampathi within the group project Smart Emergency Traffic Light Control System.

Main objective As presented in PP2

“To design, develop, and validate a multi-modal decision support system for traffic junctions that recommends the optimal green light duration by estimating queue clearance time using AI-based vehicle detection and real-time traffic analysis, achieving accurate clearance time estimation with safety buffer integration.”

The implemented prototype analyses recorded video and recommends a duration from a calibrated model; optimality has not been evaluated.

Project scope

From research gap to solution

01

Research gap

  • Detectors and density estimators give counts and classes, not a time an officer can act on.
  • Count-based timing ignores vehicle length: 2.0 m for a motorcycle, 10.8 m for a bus.
  • Discharge depends on queue position and must respect first-in-first-out order.
  • Mixed Sri Lankan traffic, including tuk-tuks, needs locally calibrated speeds.
02

Research problem

  • How can the queue seen by a calibrated fixed camera be turned into a green-light duration an officer can use?
  • How should the estimate account for vehicle class, queue position and order?
  • How can the result stay explainable and honest about its uncertainty?
03

Developed solution

  • Detection: seven-class YOLOv8n with a camera-view check and a lane-region filter.
  • Queue model: ordered queue, cumulative distance, calibrated class and position speeds, FIFO.
  • Recommendation: clearance + 3.12 s data-derived margin, with an ambulance advisory.
  • Workflow: officer web application with results and history.
