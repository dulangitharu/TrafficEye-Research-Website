Literature survey

What existing research has done, based on the literature review in the March 2026 proposal and the queue-discharge source used by the final model.

Congestion at junctions gets worse when signals use fixed timing, because a fixed timer cannot respond to the vehicles actually waiting [3][7]. Camera-based monitoring is attractive because one camera can observe many vehicles at once and supports counting, classification and density estimation [3]. 02

Research gap & problem

From research gap to the developed solution.

01

Research gap

  • A detector gives counts and classes, not a time an officer can act on.
  • Vehicle lengths run from 2.0 m (motorcycle) to 10.8 m (bus), so equal counts can mean very different queue lengths.
  • Vehicles at the front of a queue move off differently from those behind them.
  • In a single lane, a vehicle cannot clear before the one in front.
  • Local mixed traffic, including tuk-tuks, needs speeds measured on local video.
02

Research problem

  • Fixed-time or count-based green intervals do not reflect how long a particular queue needs to clear.
  • Two queues with the same number of vehicles can differ greatly in length and mix.
  • How can the queue observed by a calibrated fixed camera be turned into a recommended green duration that reflects vehicle class, queue position and order?
03

Developed solution

  • Detection: YOLOv8n, seven classes, with a camera-view check and a lane-region filter.
  • Ordered queue: vehicles ordered by distance to the reference line; groups P1, P2, P3–4, P5+.
  • Clearance model: cumulative distance ÷ calibrated speed, with a FIFO rule.
  • Recommendation: clearance + 3.12 s data-derived safety margin, plus an ambulance advisory.

How the research direction evolved

  1. 1

    Proposal, March 2026 Original proposal

    A four-camera traffic density detection component on a Raspberry Pi, with thermal support, feeding the group's cloud system.

  2. 2

    Progress Presentation 1

    Density monitoring and decision support from uploaded video: YOLOv8 detection, CLEAR / LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH levels, queue length, clearance time and a suggested green duration.

  3. 3

    Progress Presentation 2: final direction

    TrafficEye: FIFO queue ordering, clearance-time calculation with a 1.177 m standstill gap and a safety buffer, and a green-light recommendation for officers.

Research gap as stated in the original proposal
  • No practical, affordable camera-based density component that monitors all four approaches of a four-way junction in real time [2][5][9][11].
  • Reliability under poor visibility, where few systems include a thermal or other fallback [6][12].
  • Practical implementation on lightweight edge devices such as Raspberry Pi [1][2][4].

The four-camera, edge and thermal elements were not implemented in the final component.

03

Research objectives

The main objective as presented in PP2, the specific objectives that follow from it, and the individual contribution.

Overall main objective As presented in PP2

“To design, develop, and validate a multi-modal decision support system for traffic junctions that recommends the optimal green light duration by estimating queue clearance time using AI-based vehicle detection and real-time traffic analysis, achieving accurate clearance time estimation with safety buffer integration.”

How the prototype relates to this wording: it recommends a green duration from a calibrated queue-clearance model; optimality was not evaluated. It analyses uploaded recorded video rather than a live feed, and validation so far is a three-case prototype check plus a backend acceptance audit.

Original proposal objectives (March 2026)

Main objective: “To design, develop, and evaluate a camera based traffic density detection component for a four way junction that can identify vehicles, count them, and estimate real time traffic density accurately enough to support intelligent traffic signal decisions, while also exploring a thermal support approach to improve vehicle detection reliability under poor visibility conditions such as low light, rain, fog, or haze.”

  1. To capture live traffic video from four cameras placed at the four incoming roads of the junction.
  2. To develop a vehicle detection and counting method using computer vision models on a Raspberry Pi.
  3. To estimate traffic density for each road direction in real time and send the processed results to the cloud.
  4. To explore a thermal support approach for improving detection reliability under poor visibility conditions.
  5. To evaluate the component using accuracy, processing speed, and communication performance measures.
  6. To integrate the traffic density output with the wider Smart Emergency Traffic Light Control System.

Four-camera capture, Raspberry Pi processing, cloud transmission, thermal support and group integration were not implemented in the final component.

User requirements addressed in Progress Presentation 1
  1. Traffic officer can upload recorded traffic videos.
  2. System can detect and count vehicles automatically.
  3. Traffic density levels are shown clearly.
  4. Queue length and clearance time are calculated.
  5. Suggested green signal duration supports officer decisions.
  6. Analysis records and location details are stored for review.

How each part is built

01

Recorded video input and camera verification

The officer uploads a recorded video with its date and time. Before any detection, the video is matched against the calibrated camera views so that no result is guessed for an uncalibrated view.

Technologies

FastAPIOpenCV ORBRANSACroi_config.jsonroi_config_profile_b.json

Process

  1. Upload recorded video with date and time; location, road, direction and notes are optional.
  2. Compare frames with two calibrated reference views using ORB features and RANSAC.
  3. If no view matches, stop with “calibration required”.
  4. Otherwise load that view's lane polygon and reference line.
02

YOLOv8 vehicle detection and seven-class classification

YOLOv8n was trained on the Roboflow “Vehicle detection” dataset v2 (CC BY 4.0) and chosen over YOLOv5n in a comparison with matched settings, because its boxes fit vehicles more tightly (higher mAP50-95), which matters for the lane-polygon test and line crossings.

Technologies

YOLOv8nUltralytics 8.4.xPyTorch 2.11Google Colab (T4)Roboflow v2

Process

  1. Train on 3,081 images (2,174 / 599 / 308) with 6,732 labelled vehicles.
  2. Compare YOLOv5n and YOLOv8n: same data, 50 epochs, 640 px, batch 16.
  3. Detect Ambulance, Bicycle, Bus, Car, Motorcycle, Truck and Tuktuk.
  4. Run on every 10th frame at confidence 0.4 (0.25 and 0.30 rejected: classes changed abnormally between frames).

03

Region of interest and ordered vehicle queue

Only vehicles inside the calibrated lane polygon are treated as queued. The queue is taken from a single snapshot and ordered from the reference line backwards.

Technologies

OpenCV polygon testmark_roi.pyLane polygonReference line

Process

  1. Keep a vehicle if the bottom-centre of its box is inside the lane polygon.
  2. Take the sampled frame with the most vehicles in the region as the queue snapshot.
  3. Order vehicles by distance to the reference line; position 1 is closest.
  4. Assign each vehicle a position group: P1, P2, P3–4 or P5+.
04

Offline tracking and calibration

Discharge speeds and the safety margin come from a separate calibration study run beforehand on recorded local traffic. Tracking is used only here and for validation, not during normal analysis.

Technologies

ByteTrack (Ultralytics)NumPypandasMatplotlibtrack_crossings.pybuild_final_lookup_table.py

Process

  1. Record 11 clips at one fixed site (41,966 frames, about 27 minutes).
  2. Mark the lane polygon and reference lines.
  3. Track every vehicle; log reference-line crossings and queue positions.
  4. Observed clearance = crossing time − first seen in queue; speed = distance ÷ time.
  5. Remove outliers (time < 0.5 s or speed > 15 m/s).
  6. Median speed per class (7) × position group (4) → 28-row lookup table.
  7. Gaps between crossings → 3.12 s safety margin.
05

Queue distance and effective discharge speed

Each vehicle's distance to clear the reference line grows with the lengths of the vehicles ahead of it plus the standstill gaps. Its speed comes from the calibrated lookup table for its class and position group.

Technologies

traffic_calculations.pyfinal_green_time_lookup_table.csv

Process

  1. Assign each class a representative length.
  2. Compute cumulative distance Di with a 1.177 m standstill gap (Gunarathne et al., 2023).
  3. Look up V(class, group): the median effective speed from calibration.
Di = (L1 + … + Li) + (i − 1) × 1.177 m
veff = D ÷ tobsone observed vehicle
V(class, group) = median of veff

Real observations cover 15 of the 28 class and position groups, and only queue positions 1 to 4 were observed. Other groups, including P5+, rest on fallback values.

06

FIFO clearance and green-time recommendation

Raw clearance times are made consistent with single-lane order, the last vehicle's time gives the queue clearance, and the data-derived safety margin is added.

Technologies

traffic_calculations.pyanalyze_safety_buffer.py

Process

  1. Traw,i = Di ÷ V(classi, groupi).
  2. Tfifo,i = max(Traw,i, Tfifo,i−1).
  3. Queue clearance = Tfifo of the last queued vehicle.
  4. Safety margin = P85 − median of 289 crossing gaps = 6.02 − 2.90 = 3.12 s (bounds 3–8 s).
  5. Recommended green = queue clearance + 3.12 s.
07

Ambulance priority advisory

If an ambulance is detected in the lane region, a priority advisory is calculated and shown alongside the normal recommendation. It does not replace the recommendation and does not control signals.

Technologies

YOLOv8n Ambulance classEmergencyAlert page

Process

  1. Run the same calculation up to the frontmost ambulance.
  2. Priority green = the ambulance's FIFO clearance + 3.12 s.
  3. Flag priority HIGH: “Prioritize this approach until the emergency vehicle clears.”

Reliability. Ambulance detection is unreliable on the project's camera view: with the evaluated model, false “Ambulance” detections appeared in 8 of 11 normal clips.

08

Results presentation and history

Each analysis is shown to the officer and stored. After PP1 panel feedback, the traffic level uses a fixed rule on vehicle count: 0 = CLEAR, 1–4 = LOW, 5–10 = MEDIUM, 11 or more = HIGH.

Technologies

React 18Vite 5MongoDB AtlasOpenCV + ffmpeg

Process

  1. Show the recommendation card, summary figures, vehicle breakdown and ordered queue table.
  2. Render an annotated copy of the video in the background.
  3. Save the result; History filters by traffic level, emergency status and date.
  4. Overview shows recent analyses and totals.

07

Limitations & future work

What the evidence does not yet support, followed by planned improvements. Nothing in this section is an implemented feature.

Recorded video, local machine

No live CCTV input; the prototype runs on a local machine only.

One site, one lane, daytime

One calibrated fixed camera (two views), a single lane, daytime footage; night and rain not tested.

Dataset and generalisation

Validation shares source videos with training; test split not evaluated; best epoch 49 of 50.

Ambulance false positives

False “Ambulance” detections in 8 of 11 normal clips; Overview emergency counts are mostly these.

Calibration evidence

15 of 28 groups observed, positions 1–4 only; others rely on fallback values or 37 untraceable “clip_12” rows.

Limited clearance validation

Three cases from calibration clips (MAE 2.51 s, RMSE 4.22 s).

No field or user testing

No officer testing, no response-time measurement, no field trial.

No signal or group integration

TrafficEye does not control signals; integration with the wider group system has not been built.

October model not evaluated

The cctv_v3 model installed on 6 October 2026 has no TrafficEye evaluation yet.
